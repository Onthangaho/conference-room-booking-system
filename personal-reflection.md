# Sprint 1 — Personal Reflection 
## Key Learning 
The most important thing I learned is that progress in a sprint comes from completing tasks that clearly meet acceptance criteria, not from trying to move many stories at once. Even when stories look small, details like validation, error handling, and testing take time. I also learned that a sprint needs a very clear goal. When the goal is clear, it becomes easier to decide what to work on and what to delay.

## Assumptions That Failed 
 I assumed that once development started, dependent stories would naturally move forward in parallel. This was not fully true. Some tasks were completed, but they did not immediately satisfy all acceptance criteria, which meant the story could not be marked as Done. I also assumed we could touch many stories in one sprint. In reality, this reduced focus and slowed progress on the most important work.

## Behavioural Change 
In the next sprint, I would focus on fewer stories and make sure every task directly supports the acceptance criteria. I would avoid starting new work unless the sprint goal is protected. I would also review acceptance criteria more carefully before starting tasks, to ensure that “Done” really means usable and complete.

## Professional Growth 
This sprint helped me understand the importance of discipline in Agile work. Trying to do too many things at the same time creates unfinished work and confusion. I have grown in understanding how sprint goals, acceptance criteria, and task completion must align to deliver real value. This experience will help me plan and execute future sprints more effectively.
